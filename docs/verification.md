# Verification

Each method below gives what it checks, what it does not check, and its latest result. A reader can open each run link or re-run each job. The present design has the SRAM memory and the two line helpers, the USB helper with its clocked and NRZ modes: 8x4 tiles, 60 MHz, layout run [44557589283] (commit b89bd94), test run [44589189363] and coverage run [44589189245] (commit e3e1377, same `src/` as b89bd94) and formal run [44421405029] (commit 82082a1; `src/` and `formal/` unchanged since). The signoff checks of the layout at all corners are in [signoff.md](signoff.md); the sections below that name older runs describe the method and keep their results for comparison.

## 1. RTL tests

`test/Makefile` runs fourteen cocotb modules on the RTL in `src/`: the nine below and `test_usb_dev.py`, `test_eth_dev.py`, `test_linemodel.py`, `test_i2c_tgt.py` ([protocols.md](protocols.md)) and `test_slu_clk.py` (the clocked and NRZ modes of the USB helper, [helpers.md](helpers.md) section 6). The test job of every push runs them.

- `test/test_slu.py` (42 tests): the line helpers ([helpers.md](helpers.md)). `test_slu_loop_equiv`: the receive loop RTL against its bit-exact model in every cycle (50 10BASE-T frames, 50 USB packets). Unit tests of the helper RTL through its ports at 60 MHz against references written from USB 2.0 and IEEE 802.3 (`test/slu_ref.py`): USB transmit and receive (NRZI, stuffing, CRC5 and CRC16, EOP, +/-1.5 %), 10BASE-Te transmit and receive (Manchester, CRC-32, TP_IDL, link pulses, 13.5 ns jitter, +/-100 ppm), the transmit start rules (late start, underflow), collision with its 32-bit jam, jabber after 2^22 cycles, the USB bus-reset and suspend timers. Chip tests in which a thread drives a helper through the I/O ports, the pins and the data memory: 10BASE-Te and USB transmit and receive, the helper event inputs 13 and 14 with WAIT, the USB helper as an SPI controller reading a serial flash at 15 MHz, and the USB helper as a UART at 15 Mbaud in both directions (`test_slu_chip_uart_tx`, `test_slu_chip_uart_rx`). The collision test found a 31-bit jam (fixed), the event test found that the assembler rejected inputs 13 and 14 (fixed). Twenty-three tests were written for survivors of mutation campaign 1 (`mutation/mutate.py`; each kills the mutant it was written for, checked with the harness): the loop window limits (USB half a bit, 10BASE-T acquisition window, the end of acquisition after 24 accepted edges and an edge exactly on the 3-sample limit), the RX state codes and the EOP wait on LINE, every port value after reset, LINE during and after reset, RXINFO word count per frame, the TXTIME lead limit 4, a write to a full TX buffer and a write in the cycle the transmitter takes a word, the 10BASE-Te frame end, the squelch after an idle gap, TSTAMP held from EOP to EOP, CRCA_LO and CRCA_HI after a frame, the link pulse width limits, the last DIN input, the port rules (EVMASK read-back, the configuration index, RXDATA with an empty buffer), the exact line-condition threshold, the CRC flag of a runt frame, receive overflow, a frame with TXCTRL never written, a reset inside a frame (OE and the TX outputs released at once), and a clocked-mode frame shorter than a word; `test_slu_usb_tx` also checks the idle level J before and after the frame. `test_slu_link_pulse_start` pins the start cycle of a link test pulse ([helpers.md](helpers.md) section 2).

- `test/test_holdout.py` (6 tests): hold-out trials, protocols not named on the competition page or in the design documents, written from their public documents on the unchanged instruction set and assembler. AM2302 and PDI were chosen by a rule that a reader can repeat: the first two entries of the sigrok libsigrokdecode decoder list (`decoders/`, alphabetical) whose decoder reads logic channels, that link two devices, that are not in `fw/` and not named on the competition page, that 3.3 V pins with external parts can drive, that allow a bit time of 1 us or more, and that have a public specification (ac97 and aud fail the bit-time rule; the other entries before avr_pdi decode the output of the SPI, UART or I2C decoder). `test_holdout_am2302`: `fw/am2302.s` (56 words) against a sensor model from the AM2302 data sheet (pages 3 to 5) with all sensor times scaled 0.8 to 1.2, an answer after 20 to 40 us, both data sheet examples (one with a negative temperature), no sensor, and a sensor that stops after 20 bits, at 50 and 60 MHz. `test_holdout_pdi`: `fw/pdi.s` (52 words, AVR XMEGA Program and Debug Interface) against a device model from the XMEGA AU manual section 32 (enable sequence, 10 kHz inactivity rule, characters, guard time, BREAK and ERROR state, LDS, STS, LD, ST, LDCS, STCS, KEY, REPEAT), with reset pulse widths 0.1 to 40 us and device output delays of 1 cycle to HALF - 4, at 50 and 60 MHz (PDI_CLK 1 MHz). `test_holdout_pdi_rate`: the highest PDI_CLK is 1.25 MHz at 60 MHz. `test_holdout_am2302_pdi_words`: either program fits next to the two UART programs. The AM2302 trial found a fault in the first sensor model (it released the bus after a final 0 bit, which the data sheet figures do not allow), not in the firmware. `test_holdout_onewire`: `fw/onewire.s` (1-Wire controller, Analog Devices AN126) against a device model written from the DS18B20 data sheet (Maxim 19-7487 Rev 6, pages 3, 15, 16), at five device-timing corners, at 50 and 60 MHz; every controller low and high time is checked against the data sheet limits. `test_holdout_onewire_words`: 40 words; it fits next to any three of the four example programs, not next to all four. The trial found a real limit: the application note's 60 us write-0 time has no margin against a device that samples at 60 us, so the firmware uses 65 us.

- `test/test.py` (15 tests, at 50 MHz, the clock of the first design; `test_r2_host_sck_clocks` also runs 40, 48 and 60 MHz): loads `fw/uart_tx.s` through the host SPI port with the host model `test/host_spi.py` (written from [docs/host_port.md](host_port.md), not from the RTL), reads the memory back and decodes `uo_out[4]` with a UART receiver model that does not use the processor. It checks the bit time to 1 ps against 434 cycles, every edge position, back-to-back frames for 256 bytes (`test_counter_sequence`), the chip ID, the command rules and the invalid codes (every valid code with one wrong bit, and the six codes of the valid form that are no command), the SCK limit, STOP and START, and the reset during a frame. Five tests were written for survivors of mutation campaign 1: the reserved bits of READ_STATUS (and the bytes after the last status byte), a transaction that ends inside a byte, START with the reset start addresses (thread 0 runs from address 0), a helper-driven `uo_out` pin at its idle level while the threads stop, and a chip reset after configuration (the RUN mask and the pin owners return to their reset values, so START runs no thread).
- `test/test_core.py` (16 tests): lockstep, section 2; `test_core_sample_input15` (SAMPLE of input 15 reads 0, on time and late) was written for a survivor of mutation campaign 1.
- `test/test_isa.py` (21 tests): the assembler `asm/pe_asm.py` (every instruction form against the encoding of [docs/isa.md](isa.md) section 11.2, labels, directives, errors with line numbers) and the reference model `model/pe_model.py` against isa.md (barrel order, lead and late events, horizon, pin owner, open drain, SAMPLE and WAIT), with expected values worked out by hand from the document, not from the model.
- `test/test_fw_full.py` (17 tests): the full firmware in `fw/` in the reference model `model/pe_model.py`, against protocol models in the test file that do not use the firmware or the model: UART 300 bit/s to 1 Mbit/s with a sender bit-time error of ±3.3 %, SPI controller in all four modes, SPI target in all four modes (`test_m3_spi_tgt`), I2C Standard-mode and Fast-mode against the minimum times of NXP UM10204 Rev. 7.0, Table 11 (PDF page 44), with clock stretching, repeated START, NACK, arbitration loss, input spikes, bus clear and a stuck SCL, then all four protocols together from one memory image (`test_fw_full_word_count`: 103 words; the program memory has 512).
- `test/test_flex.py` (7 tests): firmware for protocols that the instruction set was not designed for, in the reference model, against protocol models written from each specification (they import only the generic bench helpers of `test_fw_full.py`, not the firmware or the processor model). `test_flex_can`: CAN 2.0A frames both ways, arbitration loss and retry, ACK, the other node's clock +/-0.5 %, a transceiver loop delay, 4 start phases (Bosch CAN 2.0 Part A). `test_flex_jtag`: a two-device chain with IDCODE, IR and DR scans and Pause-DR, set-up, hold and TDO delay checks (TI SSYA002C). `test_flex_swd`: JTAG-to-SWD, line reset, DPIDR, SELECT, AP write with WAIT, posted AP read, a wrong write parity, the reset-state rule (Arm IHI0031H). `test_flex_ps2`: both sides at 10 to 16.7 kHz, inhibit before and after the 10th clock, command with acknowledge, 264 start phases (IBM PS/2 Technical Reference, Keyboards). `test_flex_trigger`: pulse latency and jitter from a UART start edge at 16 phases. `test_flex_i2c_char`: binary search of a target's SDA set-up limit with fw/i2c_ctl.s. `test_flex_word_count`: words of each program and the sets that fit in the 512-word program memory. Each test measures the highest correct rate and logs it; [protocols.md](protocols.md) (Flexibility) and [docs/host_port.md](host_port.md) section 14 give the results.

- `test/test_sigrok.py` (15 tests, 19 scenarios): protocol programs of `fw/` on the RTL at 60 MHz against their independent models (the checks of `test_rtl_fw.py`), each with a time window and the content that its own data gives, for the outside oracle of flex/PREREG.md section 6 item 3: the sigrok workflow (`.github/workflows/sigrok.yaml`) decodes the chip pins of the same run with sigrok-cli 0.7.2 and libsigrokdecode at the pinned commit 71f4514 (`flex/sigrok_judge.py`). A scenario passes when no annotation of a warning or error class appears, the decoded content equals the scenario's, and a copy of its waveform with the data line inverted inside a content annotation fails (negative control). Latest: 19 of 19 scenarios pass (sigrok run [44421405211], commit 82082a1). Lines that the chip leaves undriven are read with the board resistor that the scenario names; logic levels only.

- `test/test_rtl_fw.py` (15 tests): every protocol program of `fw/` on the RTL at 60 MHz, against the same independent protocol model as its model test, in closed loop on the chip pins. Each test loads the firmware through the host SPI port as a host does; a host model on the SPI pins follows the host rules of [docs/host_port.md](host_port.md) sections 9 and 10 (READ_STATUS, then PUSH and PULL from the status, SCK 10 MHz), and the reference model runs in lockstep (every pin in every cycle, every word on `HOST_MISO`, the final RUN, LATE and ERR). Protocols: UART TX and RX, SPI controller and target, I2C controller (Standard-mode and Fast-mode with clock stretching), CAN, JTAG, SWD, PS/2 host and device, the timed trigger, 1-Wire, AM2302 and PDI, one standard rate each with the worst cases of its model test that fit in about one million clock cycles. `test_rtl_four_protocols` runs four protocols at the same time on the four threads (UART TX at 460,800 bit/s, SPI controller, I2C Fast-mode with stretching, JTAG), each against its own model; `test_rtl_pull_rule` measures the PULL rule of the host port. These tests found two faults that the ideal host of the model tests cannot show: a missing host rule (the port takes the next word at the end of the byte before it, so a PULL that ends before the K-th word loses a word; now in host_port.md section 9) and a firmware fault in `fw/spi_tgt.s` (it took its first MISO word before any host can push one, so every byte came one byte late; fixed). The chip pins equal the model in every cycle of every test.

Result: 175 testcases in 14 modules, test job success (run [44589189363], commit e3e1377; the job fails when `results.xml` has a failure; its `results.xml` has no failure and no skipped test): `test.py` 15, `test_isa.py` 21, `test_core.py` 16, `test_fw_full.py` 17, `test_flex.py` 7, `test_holdout.py` 6, `test_rtl_fw.py` 15, `test_slu.py` 42, `test_slu_clk.py` 7, `test_linemodel.py` 1 (it runs the 150 pytest self-tests of the line models), `test_usb_dev.py` 5, `test_eth_dev.py` 5, `test_i2c_tgt.py` 3, `test_sigrok.py` 15. Before the two chip-level UART tests: 173 testcases (run [44557589083], commit b89bd94). Before the second group of mutation-derived tests and the link-pulse and start-level tests: 160 testcases in 14 modules (run [44421405456], commit 82082a1). Before the mutation-derived tests, the sigrok scenarios and the clocked-mode tests: 119 testcases in 12 modules (run [44266106276], commit b9e112f). Before the USB, 10BASE-T and I2C target tests: 105 testcases in 8 modules (run [44005215479], commit 33e087b); earlier on this design 99 testcases before the 6 added helper tests (run [43966684776]). Before the SRAM memory and the helpers: 90 testcases, 0 failures, 0 skipped (`results.xml` of run [43779858063], commit a668e4d). Does not check: the rate sweeps and timing-fault cases of `test_fw_full.py`, `test_flex.py` and `test_holdout.py` run in the model only; on the chip, `test_rtl_fw.py` runs one standard rate per protocol, and the chip agrees with the model in every cycle there and in the lockstep tests (section 2). ## 2. Reference model and lockstep

`model/pe_model.py` is a cycle-exact model of [docs/isa.md](isa.md). `test/test_core.py` loads each program into the RTL through the host SPI port and gives the same input pins to the RTL and the model in every clock cycle. It compares `uo_out[6:0]`, `uio_out`, `uio_oe` and every host word read on `HOST_MISO` in every cycle. In RTL simulation it also compares the state of the thread in its slot (r0 to r3, T, PC, flags, LATE, ERR, RUN). Programs: directed programs for each instruction group, and random instruction streams of 4,000 cycles with 24 fixed seeds (4 seeds at gate level). A random program that the model rejects (a read 1 to 3 cycles after a store, [docs/isa.md](isa.md) section 3.3) is replaced by the next program of the same generator.

Does not check: the line helpers, which are not in the reference model (the random programs avoid I/O ports 16 to 47; inputs 13 and 14 are 0 while no helper event is enabled); `test/test_slu.py` checks the helpers against references written from USB 2.0 and IEEE 802.3 (section 1); programs that break the store-and-read rule of isa section 3.3, because their result is not defined; the internal state at gate level (the netlist has other names), where only pins and host words are compared.

## 3. Formal proofs

The properties are in the RTL (`ifdef FORMAL`) and in the wrapper files `formal/*_fv.v`. Each proof job stops with an error if fewer property cells than written reach the solver (`select -assert-min`), so a pass cannot come from properties that synthesis removed. Run with `sby -f formal/<job>.sby`. Every push that changes `src/` or `formal/` runs all 27 jobs (below); a push of other files does not, because the proofs read nothing else; the newest result is on the [Results](https://soundgod10-droid.github.io/protocol-emulator-asic/results/) page. Earlier local runs: commit afff916 (14 November 2026; mem_full 556 s, mem_full_reset 636 s, host_port_cover 722 s) and commit 9edf37e.

Does not check: the whole chip in one proof (the jobs prove blocks: core, event slot, host port, memory, and the processor as core plus event slots in `proc.sby`, where the memory, FIFOs, helpers and input unit are free models; the pin multiplexer to the pads, the host port and the configuration registers are outside it); the latch timing (the solver models the clock as a signal that changes at each step, so the latch window is checked by static timing, section 6); the bounded jobs (bmc, cover) check only their stated depth.

Kinds of the 27 jobs: unbounded proofs by k-induction (12): `core`, `timed_out`, `host_port`, `mem`, `mem_small`, `mem_full`, `mem_full_reset`, `proc`, `proc_if`, `slu_usb`, `slu_eth`, `iso`; bounded checks (5): `sram` (bmc 9), `mem_reset` (bmc 10), `host_port_mut_id` (bmc 90, the check of the check, which must find its counterexample), `slu_teeth` (13 planted faults, bmc 24 to 36, each must find its counterexample), `iso_mut_io` (bmc 12, must find its counterexample); cover jobs (9): `core_cover`, `timed_out_cover`, `host_port_cover`, `sram_cover`, `mem_cover`, `proc_cover`, `slu_cover_usb`, `slu_cover_eth`, `iso_cover`; structural check (1): `cdc`.

Bounded response of a blocked OUT (composed from the proved properties, not a separate job): an OUT waits only while its own pin's slot is full (`src/pe_core.v`: it completes unless the pin is owned and its slot full); only the owner thread writes that slot (`proc.sby` C3), so no other thread can keep it full; the slot holds one event and is empty again after the event cycle, at most 65,536 cycles after the write (`timed_out.sby` T2, T4, T6, while the cycle counter counts, that is between STARTs). So a blocked OUT completes at most 65,536 + 4 cycles after the write that filled its slot.

Every push that changes `src/` or `formal/` runs all 27 (GitHub runs at most 20 jobs at a time) (`.github/workflows/formal.yaml`, a job matrix with OSS CAD Suite 2026-11-14; a job passes when SymbiYosys returns 0, so `host_port_mut_id.sby` passes only with its counterexample, "expect fail"). First run: [43759085016] (commit 361732b), the 14 jobs of that time: `mem_full` PASS in 750 s, `mem_full_reset` PASS in 680 s, `host_port_cover` all covers in 384 s, `host_port_mut_id` DONE (FAIL, rc=0), `cdc` PASS in 8 s; about 13 min of wall time.

## 6. Static timing at all corners

The layout flow reports timing at `nom_slow_1p08V_125C`, `nom_typ_1p20V_25C` and `nom_fast_1p32V_m40C`. The flow stops the layout job on a setup or hold violation at any of the three corners (`TIMING_VIOLATION_CORNERS` and `HOLD_VIOLATION_CORNERS` are `*` in the flow's resolved configuration, `tt_submission/resolved.json` of the run; LibreLane 3.1.0.dev4 with the CMOS5L flow settings) and on a max-slew or max-capacitance violation at any corner (`MAX_SLEW_VIOLATION_CORNERS`, `MAX_CAP_VIOLATION_CORNERS` in [src/config.json](../src/config.json)). The margins themselves are read in `55-openroad-stapostpnr/summary.rpt` and in `checks.rpt` of each corner (artifact `GDS_logs`); the layout job prints them as annotations (step `Signoff facts`). [src/signoff.sdc](../src/signoff.sdc) puts flip-flop paths and latch paths in separate groups: latch paths borrow time and report a slack of 0.000, which would hide the flip-flop margin.

Result, present layout, run [44557589283] (commit b89bd94), 60 MHz (16.667 ns):

- setup: no violation at the three corners; flip-flop margin +2.307 ns (slow), +3.265 ns (typical), +3.806 ns (fast) (first path of group `flops` in each corner's `checks.rpt`). The slow-corner worst path starts at the input port `uio_in[1]` (input delay 3.333 ns, the flow's default of 20 % of the period, which [src/signoff.sdc](../src/signoff.sdc) loads) and ends at the 10BASE-Te helper's falling-edge data-input flip-flop (`_28852_`), which captures half a period after the rising edge: the first stage of the both-edge sampling, reached through the pin-select multiplexers, not a core path. Its data is an asynchronous line input, so this check is a constraint convention; the synchronizer stages after it cover the metastability (section 7). Before the clocked-mode helper (layout run [44005215480]): +1.682 / +4.311 / +4.491 ns, with the worst slow path from the core's register select. Before the setup-margin setting of [decisions.md](decisions.md) entry 21, the slow-corner margin moved between layout runs of nearly the same netlist (+1.308, +0.740, +0.487 ns in run [43989356298]), see [signoff.md](signoff.md) section 5;
- hold: no violation; worst hold slack 0.101 ns (fast), 0.276 ns (typical), 0.590 ns (slow) (`summary.rpt`);
- max slew, max capacitance and max fanout: 0 violations at each corner (with the macros, a slew or capacitance violation at any corner stops the run).

Before the SRAM memory and the helpers (run [43712217729]): +3.841 / +8.458 / +11.202 ns setup, 8 max-fanout pins with antenna-diode loads.

Does not check: on-chip variation beyond the corner models; silicon.

## 7. Synchronizer SPICE

`spice/m2c_sync_tau.sp` (typical models, 1.2 V, 25 °C) and `spice/m2c_sync_tau_ss.sp` (slow models, 1.08 V, 125 °C) find the data-to-clock offset where the input flip-flop just fails to capture, then measure the clock-to-Q delay at offsets of 10 ps to 1 fs from that point. The slope of the delay against ln(offset) from 10 ps to 0.01 ps is the metastability time constant: 0.3728 ns over ln(1000), about 54 ps (typical), and 0.6958 ns over ln(1000), about 101 ps (slow). Below 0.01 ps the delay reaches the resolution of the simulation. Every input pin has two such stages.

Does not check: the pads, process variation (one corner each, no Monte Carlo).

## How AI tools were used

The engineer was assisted by AI agents. The agents worked through a harness that lets them operate professional engineering software through its normal user interface, as an engineer does: the waveform viewer (GTKWave), the layout viewer (KLayout), a PDF reader for standards and data sheets, and the open-source design tools named in this file. They were not limited to a command line. After a change, an agent opened the result in the same software and checked it on the screen, so that it saw what a reviewer sees, for example the waveforms of a protocol test or the layout of a gds run. The result line of a tool was not taken as the check on its own. The visual check is there to catch errors that a language model can make when it only reads and writes text.

**How the results were checked.** No result was accepted because an AI tool said so. Each design choice needed evidence that a reader can check (a test or formal job in this repository, a GitHub Actions run, a synthesis area, or a numbered page of a public standard or data sheet) and a comparison with at least one alternative; [docs/decisions.md](decisions.md) keeps the rejected options and the failed runs. The tests compare the design with models written from the documents, not from the RTL: the protocol models in `test/` follow the standards, the host model follows [docs/host_port.md](host_port.md), and the expected values in `test_isa.py` were worked out by hand from docs/isa.md. Later reviews read each cited source again. Some first answers were wrong and were corrected this way: the I2C input filter needs 4 equal samples at 50 MHz, not 3 ([docs/decisions.md](decisions.md) entry 10); the SPI target firmware is rated 1 MHz, not the 5 MHz first estimated (entry 11); the 1-Wire write-0 time of the application note (60 us) has no margin against a DS18B20 that samples at 60 us, and the hold-out test found it (section 1). The final checks are those of the flow (static timing at three corners, DRC, LVS, precheck) and the gate-level tests, not an AI tool's reading of them.
